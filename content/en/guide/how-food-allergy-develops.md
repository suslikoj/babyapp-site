# **What causes a food allergy to develop?**

## **Where could things have gone off track?**

A food allergy does not develop by chance. It is the result of a situation in which a child’s **immune system loses the ability to recognize a common food as something safe**. In medical terms, this is called a **breakdown of immune tolerance**—but simply put, the body begins to see a threat where none actually exists.

### **1.** How the immune system works in a healthy child

In most children, the immune system works in a way that it:

- **recognizes foods as harmless,**
- **learns to tolerate them,**
- creates so-called *tolerogenic immune cells* that maintain immune calm.

This tolerance is formed primarily **in the gut,** as the child consumes normal foods.

### **2. When tolerance fails – the beginning of allergy**

In children with genetic predispositions (atopic children), or under certain environmental influences, this protective “army” of tolerogenic cells can **switch into defense mode**.

Cells that promote **allergic reactions** begin to dominate.

##### What triggers the shift toward allergy?

**Genetics**  
If eczema, asthma, or allergies are present in the family, the child has a higher risk.

**The modern environment (we live differently than before)**

- more chemicals in food and everyday products,
- environmental pollution,
- fewer microbes (overly clean environments),
- highly processed foods,
- increased stress.

**Changes in the gut microbiome**

Healthy gut = healthy immunity.  
Disruption of the gut microbiota = higher risk of allergies.

**Pharmacological burden (frequent medications)**

Certain medications can shift the immune system toward increased reactivity.

**Chronic stress – even in young children**

Stress alters immune function and promotes inflammatory responses.

### **3. The dual exposure hypothesis**

Research shows that children can become **sensitized to foods through the skin**—especially when they have:

- a disrupted skin barrier (eczema),
- dry, cracked skin,
- frequent contact with foods via an adult’s hands during handling or feeding.

**If an allergenic food enters the body through the skin before it is introduced orally, the risk of developing an allergy increases.**

This is also why current recommendations emphasize:

- **early introduction of foods into the diet** (so tolerance develops in the gut),
- **careful management of eczema.**

Because:

**When a child comes into contact with an allergenic food through damaged skin, the immune system may develop an allergy even before the child has ever tasted the food.**

### Summary

**Allergy is not a parental failure—it is the result of many interacting factors that are often beyond full control.** The risk of allergy can be reduced to some extent by supporting a healthy diet, minimizing toxic exposures, ensuring adequate sleep, and promoting emotional well-being.  
Still, allergies can develop even when everything is done “right.”

The good news is that today we know how to work with food allergies, and in many cases it is possible to gradually support their **fading**.
