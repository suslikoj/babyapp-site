# Low-risk foods

## **How to maintain variety and calm during the diet?**

With an elimination diet, it is very easy to slip into the feeling that “there is nothing I can eat.”  
In reality, however, there is a **wide range of foods with low allergenic potential** that can be used to create a **complete and varied diet**—even during stricter phases of the diet.

### **General principles**

- Safe foods have **a low allergenic potential**, but they can still be problematic on an individual level.
- Most foods are **better tolerated when cooked** rather than raw.
- Variety is more important than relying on just a few “safe staples.”
- Long-term reliance on a very limited selection of foods **is not the goal** and may do more harm than good.

### **Vegetables – the foundation of a safe diet**

Vegetables are the most important component of the diet in the context of allergies.  
They support gut healing, reduce inflammation, and help stabilize the immune system.

Vegetables that are usually well tolerated (especially **when cooked**) include:

- potatoes, sweet potatoes, pumpkin,
- zucchini, pattypan squash, eggplant,
- broccoli, cauliflower, kohlrabi,
- Brussels sprouts, cabbage (white, red, napa),
- green peas, green beans,
- spinach, chard, leafy greens,
- cucumber,
- beetroot,
- parsnip.

Vegetables can be served:

- in soups (both creamy soups and broths),
- as side dishes,
- in porridges or purées,
- baked, stewed, or roasted.

### **Fruit**

Fruit is generally well tolerated, especially:

- apples, pears,
- peaches, apricots, plums, pluots,
- cherries, sour cherries,
- melons,
- grapes,
- blueberries,
- avocado.

For more sensitive children, it is recommended to:

- start with cooked fruit (boiled or baked),
- gradually try fresh fruit.

### **Meat**

Meat is an important source of protein and iron and **should not be eliminated unnecessarily**.

Usually well tolerated are:

- pork,
- rabbit,
- lamb, mutton,
- game meat,
- goose, duck.

Processed meats are not considered safe foods—they often contain additives that can worsen eczema.

### **Grains and pseudograins**

Even without wheat and oats, there are many safe options available:

- rice,
- corn,
- buckwheat,
- millet,
- quinoa, amaranth,
- sorghum,
- rye and barley (often well tolerated and important for maintaining contact with gluten).

They can be used:

- as side dishes,
- in the form of porridges,
- flakes,
- groats or polenta,
- in soups.

### **Beverages**

Suitable beverages include:

- water,
- mild herbal teas (fennel, caraway, linden, elderflower),
- rooibos,
- weak green tea,
- weak black tea.

### **Other foods**

Often well tolerated:

- coconut,
- high-quality oils and fats,
- mild herbs.

### **Important note**

A common mistake is that after improvement, parents remain on a very restricted diet for too long.  
This can lead to:

- nutritional deficiencies,
- disruption of the gut microbiome,
- a return of symptoms.

The goal of the entire diet is a **return to a varied diet**, not permanent elimination of foods.
