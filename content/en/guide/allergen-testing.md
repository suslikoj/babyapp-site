# **Allergen testing**

Allergen testing is a key part of **the elimination–challenge diet.**  
Its goal is not to confirm what causes problems for the child, but primarily to **identify what may no longer be problematic** and can safely return to the diet.

Thanks to testing, it is not necessary to maintain an unnecessarily restricted diet for too long, and the body has the opportunity to **learn tolerance.**

## When is the right time to start your testing journey?

- If the child’s condition has **significantly improved or symptoms have disappeared after the first phase of the diet**, you can begin testing.
- If no improvement occurred and you completed the complete elimination phase, **testing begins without waiting for further improvement**—in this case, reintroducing foods is often what helps move the condition forward.

## How does allergen testing work?

Allergens are **tested gradually, one at a time.**

The process is simple:

- choose one allergen,
- introduce it into the diet according to the recommended amount,
- observe the reaction,
- evaluate the result and only then proceed to the next one.

If the allergen **passes without a reaction**, it can be returned to the diet.  
If it causes a reaction, testing is paused and resumes only after the condition has settled, usually after about 2–3 days, with another allergen.

## Testing via breast milk and direct exposure

If you are breastfeeding, it is recommended to:

- first test the allergen **through breast milk,**
- and only if no reaction occurs, introduce the allergen **directly to the child.**

Reactions through breast milk often appear with a **delay of 12–24 hours**, sometimes even longer.  
That is why it is important to allow enough time for testing and **not rush conclusions.**

## How long should you test one allergen?

In most cases, **3 days** are sufficient:

- day one: a small amount,
- day two: a larger amount,
- day three: a full portion.

For infants and sensitive children, testing starts with **a very small amount**—sometimes just a taste is enough.  
The goal is not to provoke a strong reaction, but to recognize **whether a reaction occurs at all.**

## **What to do if you are unsure?**

If you are not sure about the test result:

- temporarily eliminate the allergen,
- **test it again** after some time.

Reactions can change over time—as the gut heals and the immune system matures, the **likelihood of tolerance increases**. It is also possible that the reaction was not caused by the allergen itself, but by an external factor (teething, illness, etc.). This can be recognized if the condition does not improve even several days after consuming the allergen.

### **Food groups**

It is often the case that children:

- do not react to individual foods in isolation,
- but to **entire food groups.**

For example, if one type of root vegetable is tolerated, there is a high chance that others will be tolerated as well.  
Even so, it is still worthwhile to **test individual foods separately** at a later stage.

## **If an allergen does not pass**

If a reaction occurs:

- stop testing immediately,
- wait for the condition to return to baseline (usually 1–3 days),
- only then continue with the next test.

A short break between tests is a normal and important part of the process.

## **Reintroducing allergens**

Allergens **that pass** **are** **reintroduced immediately** and without unnecessary restrictions.

For allergens that do not pass:

- it is not necessary to eliminate them absolutely,
- it is often possible to **offer them occasionally in small amounts**, if they do not cause a significant reaction.

Regular, controlled contact with an allergen helps the body learn tolerance—always after testing has been completed.

## **Repeat testing**

Testing makes sense to **repeat every 2–3 months.**

Very often:

- an allergen that previously did not pass begins to be tolerated,
- at least in a certain form (for example, when cooked).

Thanks to repeated testing:

- the diet gradually expands,
- dietary restrictions become less limiting,
- and the body has a real chance to overcome the allergy.

## **Do not be afraid to test**

Testing is not a mistake or a risk.  
It is a way of communicating with the body.

Without testing, the diet remains unnecessarily restricted and the body loses the opportunity to learn tolerance.  
Every small step forward is progress in the right direction and often **brings renewed motivation and hope.**
