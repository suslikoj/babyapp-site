# **Non-food influences**

## **Which affect eczema and allergy symptoms**

Eczema in children is not only influenced by diet. Very often, other factors also interfere with its course, which can significantly worsen or, conversely, improve the skin condition.

This is especially important when testing foods. If these influences are not taken into account, it can lead to incorrect assessment of the reaction and unnecessary dietary restrictions.

### **Main non-food factors**

#### **Tooth growth**

Teething is one of the most common causes of sudden worsening of eczema.

It typically manifests itself as:

- sudden worsening without a change in diet
- dry, red and often warm skin (especially on the cheeks)
- fluctuations in condition during the day
- worsening even a few days to weeks before teething

Eczema can react prematurely, which often leads to doubts and uncertainty during evaluation.

#### **Illness and weakening of the body**

Any illness poses a burden on a child's body, which can also affect the skin.

Typical manifestations:

- worsening of eczema without changing your diet
- greater fluctuations in condition
- gradual improvement after the disease has subsided

At this stage, it is not appropriate to draw conclusions about food reactions.

#### **Vaccination**

Eczema may temporarily worsen after vaccination.

It manifests itself:

- increased skin sensitivity
- a temporary worsening lasting a few days to a week

This is an immune system reaction, not a food intolerance.

#### **Weather and climate conditions**

The condition of the skin is significantly influenced by the surrounding environment.

The most common factors include:

- cold, wind and frost
- dry air
- high temperatures and overheating
- rapid weather changes

Eczema is often significantly worse in the winter, especially due to dry air and the combination of cold and heating.

#### **Humidity and sweating**

Excessive sweating and humid environments can significantly worsen eczema.

Typical manifestations:

- irritation in skin folds
- increased itching
- deterioration when overheated

Inappropriate layering of clothing is a common trigger.

#### **Environment**

A change in environment can have a significant impact on the condition of the skin.

This includes, for example:

- stay in a different environment (visits, vacation)
- air quality
- presence of dust and mites

Some children respond by significantly improving outside the home environment and then deteriorating again upon return.

#### **Mental stress, fatigue and daily fluctuations**

The condition of eczema naturally changes throughout the day.

Typical is:

- Skin is calmer in the morning
- eczema tends to be more pronounced in the evening and before bed
- When tired, the skin tends to be more red and "inflamed"

This fluctuation is common and does not automatically mean a reaction to the food. It is important to assess the condition of the skin in the context of the entire day, ideally at the same time.

### **How to distinguish between a food reaction and a non-food influence**

When testing foods, it is essential to monitor the development of the condition after their withdrawal.

Typical food reaction:

- improvement occurs after eliminating the allergen
- the skin returns to the state it was in before the reaction within 2–3 days

If the condition does not improve:

- the worsening persists for more than a few days
- the condition fluctuates or does not return to its original state

it is likely that this is a non-food influence.

He can:

- last a few days, but also a few weeks
- subside only after the trigger disappears

Once this effect wears off, the skin condition usually returns.

### **Practical experience**

In practice, it is very common for a parent to be unable to distinguish what caused the deterioration. A typical example is teething — the skin condition worsens for no apparent reason, persists for several days, and raises doubts about the diet.

Subsequently, the tooth appears and the condition returns. Such an experience is not an exception.

### **Testing during deterioration**

Food testing is possible even during periods when non-food influences affect the skin, but certain specifics need to be taken into account:

- the reaction may be more pronounced because the organism is burdened by multiple factors
- deterioration may be more intense

Nevertheless, in most cases it is better to continue testing and not extend the break unnecessarily.

A long interruption in testing can significantly slow down the entire process.

### **Why it is important to know these factors**

Without knowledge of these influences, incorrect evaluation may occur:

- unnecessary food waste
- prolonging the elimination diet
- feeling that no procedure is working

In reality, however, the cause may not be in the food.

### **Summary**

Eczema in children is influenced not only by food, but also by a number of non-food factors, such as teething, illness, vaccinations, environment, weather, or fatigue.

A typical sign of a food reaction is that the skin returns to its original condition within 2-3 days after the allergen is removed. If the worsening persists or fluctuates, it is likely that the cause lies outside the diet.

Moreover, the condition of the skin naturally changes during the day - it is more noticeable in the evening and when tired, which always needs to be taken into account when assessing.

A correct assessment therefore requires monitoring the context over time and evaluating multiple factors simultaneously.
