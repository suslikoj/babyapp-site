Phase 2 of the elimination diet: complete elimination

In short

Complete elimination follows when a child’s condition hasn’t improved after the first phase. It is the strictest phase but short – 4–5 days, 7 at most. Vegetables are the foundation, along with allowed fruit, meat and grains. Allergen testing follows straight after.

You enter this phase when the child’s condition has not improved after the first phase, has improved only minimally, or has even worsened. This means that the trigger of the symptoms is likely not limited to the main suspects.

For a few days, you eliminate all potential allergens and then move directly to testing.

How long this phase lasts?

This phase is the strictest, but also short. It lasts 4–5 days, with a maximum of 7 days. Never longer.

Long-term elimination of a large number of foods is not the goal and may be counterproductive.

What matters most in this phase?

During complete elimination, you:

  • follow the principles of the elimination diet very strictly,
  • focus on simple, nourishing meals,
  • ensure sufficient intake of green vegetables,
  • support the gut microbiome (for example with appropriate probiotics).

As soon as the condition improves, you do not wait—you move immediately to allergen testing and the gradual reintroduction of foods.

What foods can you include during this phase?

Vegetables – the foundation of the entire phase

Vegetables are absolutely key during this phase. They help reduce inflammation, support the gut, detoxification, and overall calming of allergic symptoms.

This includes especially:

  • leafy and green vegetables (various types of lettuce, kale, chard, spinach),
  • broccoli, cauliflower, kohlrabi, zucchini, cucumber,
  • green peas, green beans,
  • onion, leek, garlic,
  • pumpkin, sweet potatoes, potatoes,
  • beetroot, fennel, ginger,
  • herbs (basil, parsley, chives, dill, lovage, and others).

Vegetables can be eaten raw or cooked, in soups, porridges, purées, side dishes, salads, or added to meals and drinks.

Fruit

The following fruits are mainly allowed:

  • apples, pears,
  • peaches, nectarines, apricots, plums, pluots,
  • cherries, sour cherries,
  • melons,
  • grapes,
  • dates, figs,
  • avocado.

Fruit can be served fresh, cooked, stewed, baked, dried, or in the form of purées, jams, or syrups. For babies, you may prefer to start with cooked fruit for greater safety.

Meat

Meat still has its place in this phase—it is an important source of protein, iron, and other nutrients.

Especially suitable are:

  • rabbit,
  • pork,
  • mutton, lamb,
  • duck, goose,
  • game meat.

Grains and pseudograins

Even without wheat and oats, you still have many options:

  • rice,
  • buckwheat, quinoa, amaranth,
  • millet, sorghum,
  • corn,
  • rye and barley (it is recommended not to eliminate them completely—they help maintain contact with gluten).

They can be used:

  • as side dishes,
  • in the form of porridges,
  • flakes,
  • groats, polenta,
  • in soups.

Foods that are not ideal, but acceptable in small amounts

Some foods are not nutritionally beneficial, but in reasonable amounts it may not cause harm:

  • cookies, chips, sweets (pay close attention to ingredients),
  • extruded snacks, puffs,
  • mineral waters, store-bought juices,
  • one small cup of coffee per day (or grain coffee).

These foods do not replace полноцен meals, but small occasional amounts can make the diet easier to manage.

Processed meats

Processed meats are not suitable in this phase. They often contain additives that can worsen eczema or trigger reactions.

If you need meat for bread, it is better to:

  • prepare homemade roasted meat,
  • use a quality fat (for example lard),
  • and simply slice or spread the meat.

What will follow?

After just a few days, you begin testing—even if there has not been a significant improvement. Improvement may occur only after the correct foods are reintroduced, and this will be identified in the next phase. Strict elimination does not continue further.

The allergen testing phase follows, in which foods are systematically and carefully reintroduced into the diet.

The app will guide you through the entire process and help you identify the true causes of the difficulties.

Frequently asked questions

How long does phase 2 of the elimination diet last?

4–5 days, 7 days at most, never longer. Removing lots of foods long-term is not the goal and can be counterproductive.

What can you eat during complete elimination?

Mainly vegetables (leafy and green, broccoli, zucchini, pumpkin, potatoes), fruit such as apples, pears or apricots, meat such as rabbit, pork, lamb or duck, and grains such as rice, buckwheat, millet or corn. See Low-risk foods for more.

What if there is no improvement even after complete elimination?

After a few days you start allergen testing anyway. Improvement may only come once the right foods are back – strict elimination does not continue.

Can I eat processed meats on an elimination diet?

Not in this phase – they often contain additives that can make eczema worse. Home-roasted meat is a better choice.
