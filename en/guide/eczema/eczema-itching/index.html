Itching in eczema: what helps during the day and at night

In short

Itching isn’t naughtiness but the body’s natural response to inflammation. During the day, distraction, zinc ointments and wet wraps help; at night, co-sleeping, breastfeeding, covered hands and a cooler room. When the itching doesn’t ease, it’s time to look for the cause.

What helps during the day and at night

Itching is one of the most challenging symptoms of eczema. It exhausts both the child and the parents and very often worsens in the evening and at night. It is important to understand that itching is not misbehavior or a bad habit—it is a natural reaction of the body.

It cannot be forbidden. The goal is to relieve discomfort, protect the skin, and look for the underlying cause.

Why eczema itches

Itching arises from a combination of several factors:

  • the release of histamine, often in connection with an allergic reaction to foods,
  • overall strain on the body—during illness, teething, after vaccination, or with general exhaustion,
  • inflammation in the skin and hypersensitive nerve endings,
  • healing skin can also itch, which may feel like worsening even when the condition is actually improving.

Daytime tips – How to manage itching during the day

Do not forbid scratching—redirect It

Forbidding scratching increases stress and often leads to an even stronger reaction.

A better approach is to:

  • redirect attention (play, cuddling, singing),
  • keep the hands busy with another stimulus,
  • with younger children, gently hold the hands without force or struggle.

Zinc ointments

  • dry, soothe, and cool the skin,
  • are suitable for redness,
  • can be either store-bought or homemade (with the simplest possible ingredients).

Apply locally in a thin layer.

Wet wraps during flare-ups

Very effective for intense itching:

  • agrimony or chamomile tea (lukewarm),
  • soak a cloth diaper or cotton fabric,
  • apply for 5–10 minutes,
  • then allow the skin to breathe freely.

Natural “antihistamines”

This is not treatment, but gentle support:

  • for example, rooibos tea,
  • always monitor the child’s individual response.

Think about the cause

If itching keeps recurring, it is important to look for the cause:

  • is the trigger an external factor (teething, illness, environment) or diet (unhealthy foods, allergens)?

If the cause cannot be identified, a systematic approach is needed rather than random elimination.

Nighttime tips – when itching is the worst

At night, cortisol levels drop and itching often becomes significantly worse. The goal is fast relief and protection of the skin.

Co-sleeping

Parental closeness:

  • calms the child,
  • allows for a quick response,
  • often prevents intense scratching.

Breastfeeding as relief

Breastfeeding:

  • calms the nervous system,
  • provides comfort even without hunger,
  • many mothers know the feeling of a “24/7 milk bar”—and that is okay.

“Little Mummy” method

If the child scratches intensely at night:

  • gently wrap the arms close to the body (for example, with a light blanket),
  • make sure the child cannot roll over.

Covering the hands

  • special clothing designed for children with eczema,
  • cotton socks placed over the hands.

Coolness and calm

  • a slightly cooler room,
  • breathable clothing,
  • if needed, a short wet compress even in the evening.

Care for scratched skin

If scratching does occur:

  1. gently clean and disinfect the area,
  2. dragon’s blood resin can then be used to support healing.

It is important to prevent infection.

When to address itching “From the inside”

If:

  • itching does not improve and instead worsens,
  • the child is eating a healthy, nutritious diet,
  • there is no ongoing illness,
  • the child is not teething,

then it is appropriate to consider an elimination–challenge diet as the next step in identifying the true cause.

Summary

  • Itching is a natural reaction of the body to inflammation.
  • Histamine, overall bodily strain, and allergic reactions play a key role.
  • Forbidding scratching does not work—protection and redirection do.
  • Nighttime is the most difficult, but there are ways to protect the skin.
  • If the condition does not improve, it is essential to search for the cause systematically.

Frequently asked questions

Why is eczema itchier at night?

Cortisol levels drop at night and itching often gets much worse.

What helps eczema itching in a baby?

A thin layer of zinc ointment, wet wraps with lukewarm agrimony or chamomile tea for 5–10 minutes, distraction and, at night, covered hands (cotton mittens or special eczema clothing).

Should I stop my child from scratching?

No. Forbidding it raises stress and often makes the reaction stronger. It’s better to redirect – with play, cuddles or gently holding the hands.

How do I care for scratched eczema?

Gently clean and disinfect the area; dragon’s blood can be used to support healing. The key is preventing infection.
