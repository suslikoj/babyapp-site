Different skin rashes in babies: not everything is eczema

In short

Not every red patch is atopic eczema. Babies often get diaper rash, seborrheic dermatitis (cradle cap), contact reactions or bacterial impetigo. If the rash keeps coming back, doesn’t respond to usual care or comes with diarrhea or restlessness, it’s worth looking for a deeper cause.

Not everything is eczema

The skin of young children is immature, thin and sensitive. It is therefore not uncommon for various skin manifestations to appear in the first months of life, which may at first glance resemble eczema, allergies or infections. However, in reality, these are often temporary or specific skin reactions that have a different cause and solution.

Correct differentiation is key – not every red spot is atopic eczema and not every rash is related to food. However, some symptoms may be the first signal of an overworked immune system or the onset of an allergic reaction.

Diaper rash (diaper dermatitis)

Diaper rash is one of the most common skin problems in infants. It is caused by a combination of:

  • humidity,
  • friction,
  • contact with urine and feces,
  • disrupted skin barriers.

They typically appear in the diaper area – on the buttocks, thighs, and in skin folds.

Cold sores may just be a skin problem, but if they recur, heal poorly, or spread, it may signal:

  • congested intestine,
  • reaction to food ingredients (allergy),
  • reaction to antibiotics.

In this case, it is no longer just a local skin problem.

Impetigo (bacterial skin infection)

Impetigo is a superficial bacterial infection of the skin, most often caused by staphylococci or streptococci. It manifests itself in:

  • wet bearings,
  • typical yellow to honey-colored scabs,
  • rapid spread.

It often occurs on damaged skin - for example, at the site of eczema, rashes or after scratching.

Impetigo is more common in children with eczema because:

  • the skin barrier is weakened,
  • the skin is more susceptible to infection.

In this case, diet or lubrication alone cannot be the solution – the infection must be managed first. If you suspect something is wrong, see a pediatrician or dermatologist who will take a swab and, based on the results, prescribe treatment.

Seborrheic dermatitis (cradle cap)

Seborrhea typically appears:

  • on the hairy part of the head,
  • in the eyebrows,
  • behind the ears,
  • sometimes even in the diaper area.

It manifests itself:

  • greasy, yellowish scales,
  • usually without significant itching.

Seborrheic dermatitis is related to:

  • immaturity of the sebaceous glands,
  • hormonal influences,
  • sometimes with microbial colonization of the skin.

However, in some children, seborrhea can turn into atopic eczema over time, especially if there is a family history or other signs of hypersensitivity.

Contact skin reactions

It occurs through direct skin irritation:

  • detergents,
  • fabric softener,
  • cosmetics,
  • wet wipes,
  • some materials (synthetics, metals).

The symptoms are:

  • sharply demarcated,
  • at the point of contact,
  • often without other systemic symptoms,
  • if contact is not repeated, it disappears,

These reactions are not a food allergy, but they occur more frequently in children with eczema or allergic burden.

When to sharpen your wits and look for a deeper cause

Situations that deserve increased attention include:

  • skin manifestations recur,
  • they do not respond to normal measures,
  • worsen after eating, illness, or vaccination,
  • are accompanied by eczema, diarrhea, reflux or restlessness in the child.

In these cases, the skin often no longer reacts on its own, but reflects the state of intestinal immunity and the overall burden on the organism.

How to approach these manifestations

If we are not sure whether a child really has atopic eczema, it is not advisable to immediately embark on strict elimination diets. Many skin manifestations in babies are temporary, related to the immaturity of the skin, intestines or immune system and can resolve themselves with a basic change in regimen.

The first step should always be:

  • return to a simple, natural diet,
  • limiting industrially processed foods,
  • emphasis on quality fats, proteins and basic foods,
  • calming the environment (household chemicals, cosmetics, detergents).

In many children, skin manifestations improve simply by not putting unnecessary strain on the body.

When does it make sense to move on?

If skin manifestations:

  • they repeat,
  • persist,
  • worsen,
  • or other problems are added to them (eczema, digestion, restlessness, diarrhea),

then it makes sense to proceed to the next step – a controlled elimination-exposure diet.

It is not about eliminating "everything" across the board, but a systematic and temporary approach that helps:

  • identify specific triggers,
  • distinguish a true allergy from a transient hypersensitivity,
  • while protecting dietary diversity and microbiome development.

The app helps with this step - in a structured and safe way, especially for the youngest children, where allergens in the diet often play a crucial role.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell cradle cap from eczema?

Seborrhea (cradle cap) shows as greasy, yellowish scales mainly on the scalp, eyebrows and behind the ears and usually doesn’t itch much. In some children, however, it can later turn into atopic eczema.

What is impetigo and when should I see a doctor?

A superficial bacterial skin infection with weeping patches and yellow to honey-colored crusts that spreads quickly. If you suspect it, see your pediatrician or a dermatologist – they will take a swab and decide on treatment.

Why does diaper rash keep coming back?

Recurring or slow-healing diaper rash can point to an overloaded gut, a reaction to something in the diet (allergy) or a reaction to antibiotics.

Should I start an elimination diet as soon as a rash appears?

No. The first step is simple, natural food and a calmer environment (household chemicals, cosmetics, detergents). An elimination–challenge diet makes sense when the rash keeps coming back, persists or other problems appear.
